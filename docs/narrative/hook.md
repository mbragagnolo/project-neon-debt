# The hook

## Premise — LOCKED

Your body was rebuilt on a standard finance plan. You didn't choose most of
the chrome, and you are upside down on the loan. The Stacks is where people
like you go: a vertical warren of write-offs the corp stopped servicing
years ago. Then the corp sold the district's debt book to a collections
outfit, and their enforcer — **the Landlord** — started working the towers
floor by floor. You are not a hero. You are behind on payments, and he has
reached your floor. The slice ends with him coming to collect *you*; winning
buys time, not freedom.

## Ability gates are feature-locked firmware — LOCKED

The metroidvania gates are not missing augs. The hardware is already in your
body — **disabled pending payment**, like heated seats on a subscription.
Every visible-but-unreachable ledge is a paywall: "how do I get up there?"
has an answer the player can feel in their wallet. M5 dresses every
double-jump tease accordingly (lock glyphs, firmware-tier signage — greybox
can do this with text).

## The unlock is stolen authority, never money — LOCKED

Enforcement of the firmware locks is delegated: the Landlord's deck carries
the **collections override** — the tool that remotely *disables* a
defaulter's augs, which is why The Stacks fears a man the towers could
otherwise gang up on. Beating him grants no money and no forgiveness: you
rip the override module out of his chrome. The corp's instrument for
switching debtors off becomes yours for switching yourself on.

- The slice's closing shot, made literal: the ledge lights up because your
  leg firmware just accepted an unauthorized override. V2 opens with you
  cracking the first lock — the double jump is **cracked, not granted**.
- Credits never touch an ability unlock, in fiction or mechanics — a player
  who spends everything can never be softlocked out of progression.
- Fits the Firewall→Overload arc (hacks.md): endure with their program,
  steal your first weapon, finally steal the keys.
- Parked V2 boss idea: the Landlord attempts the remote-disable on the
  player mid-fight.

## Protagonist — LOCKED

**Talks, Castlevania-style.** Written lines at story beats — first save
terminal, finding Overload, the quest turn-in, pre/post boss — never a
dialogue tree, never a choice of words (consistent with the no-speech-checks
lock; systems stay combat-only). Voice: terse, tired, working-class wry.
Budget discipline: a handful of lines in the whole slice; scarcity is what
makes them land. Name `TODO` — revealed the way the corp would print it, on
paperwork.

## Tone — LOCKED

**Working-class cyberpunk.** Paycheck dread, not neon-samurai glamour. The
horror is ordinary — compound interest, terms of service, a repossession
notice — and the humor is gallows-adjacent. Story lives in environments,
short barks, and documents; the debt statement is the genre's scariest
artifact.

## The quest, flavored — LOCKED

The body in the hazard area is a defaulter who ran. The memory chip is what
he didn't want repossessed with the rest of him. The neighbor who sends you
is the only person who remembers him as a person. The reward is his hardhat
(already locked in items.md placement) — you inherit the dead man's safety
equipment, and it is the best helmet in the game.

## Naming conventions — LOCKED (names TODO)

- **Tools:** worn brand-and-model names, manufacturer first.
- **Hacks:** program names — Firewall / Overload / Breach already comply.
- **Enemies:** Stacks slang — Scav already complies; Watcher, Riot unit and
  Elite pass.
- **The corp:** ONE name, used everywhere — augs, save terminals, the
  Landlord's paperwork. A debt story needs a creditor with a face.
- **Save terminals:** corp property, branded as such — you heal and save on
  the creditor's own infrastructure.
